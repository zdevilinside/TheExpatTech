# Tech & News Roundup — Week 8, 2026

After taking a few weeks off to deal with some health issues and personal matters, I’m back to the weekly format. The goal remains the same as before: filter the signal from the noise and highlight the stories that actually say something about where technology — and sometimes the world — is heading.

## Audio & Home Theater

### How to Use Your HDMI eARC Port (Pocket-Lint)
**URL:** https://www.pocket-lint.com/how-to-use-your-hdmi-earc-port/

The HDMI eARC port is designed to send high-bandwidth audio from your TV to external audio gear like soundbars and AV receivers, supporting formats such as Dolby TrueHD and DTS-HD Master Audio.

**Commentary:**  
eARC fixes most of ARC’s reliability and bandwidth limitations, but interoperability between TVs, receivers, and HDMI cables can still be messy. When it works, it’s effectively transparent lossless audio from any TV source. When it doesn’t, you end up power-cycling half your living room.  I am going to have to rethink how I usually set things up when I redo my home theater setup in the future.

### Optoma UHZ36 Laser Projector Launch
**URL:** https://www.androidauthority.com/optoma-uhz36-launch-3639631/

A compact 4K laser projector emphasizing brightness, longevity, and low input lag for gaming.

**Commentary:**  
Laser projection is crossing from enthusiast territory into mainstream pricing. The trade-off remains contrast performance versus OLED TVs, but for large-screen viewing without a permanent install, the convenience is hard to beat.  I am going to be putting a projector in at my new home shortly because my use case demands it.

## Storage & Hardware

### Western Digital’s 14-Platter HAMR HDD Designs (Tom’s Hardware)
**URL:** https://www.tomshardware.com/news/wd-14-platter-hamr-hdd

Western Digital outlined a roadmap toward extremely high-capacity HAMR drives exceeding 100 TB in the coming decade.

**Commentary:**  
HAMR has been perpetually “almost ready,” but hyperscale demand is forcing it into reality. These drives are aimed squarely at data centers, not consumers — think cold storage for AI training datasets rather than desktop upgrades.  Personally, I am starting to wonder if I can afford to upgrade my NAS with larger drives with how quickly the costs are going up.  

---

### AI Has Bought Out Western Digital’s 2026 HDD Supply
**URL:** https://mashable.com/article/ai-hard-drive-hdd-shortages-western-digital-sold-out

Hyperscalers are reportedly absorbing nearly all high-capacity HDD production for AI infrastructure.

**Commentary:**  
AI doesn’t just need GPUs — it needs massive storage for datasets, checkpoints, and logs. Spinning disks remain the cheapest way to store petabytes, so they’re quietly becoming strategic infrastructure.  It is sad to see the consumer get prices jacked up like it is right now.  I don't know what I would do if I didn't have a supply of RAM and hard drives on the shelf right now.

### Soldered RAM Is Coming to Desktops
**URL:** https://www.xda-developers.com/soldered-ram-is-here-for-laptops-and-desktops-are-next/

LPDDR memory is beginning to appear in compact desktop systems.

**Commentary:**  
Manufacturers gain efficiency and lower power consumption; users lose upgradeability. Expect this trend to accelerate in mini-PCs and all-in-ones first.  I do not like soldered on RAM because it locks you into that amount forever unless you change motherboards in the future.  It is a bad move in the industry in my opinion but some will see it is a huge benefit.  When I look at the cost increases for increasing RAM in soldered situations, my stomach turns.

### RAM Shortage Convinced Me to Learn Memory Overclocking
**URL:** https://www.xda-developers.com/ram-shortage-convinced-me-to-learn-memory-overclocking/

High demand — partly driven by AI hardware — has pushed memory prices upward.

**Commentary:**  
DDR5 tuning has matured enough that manual optimization can yield meaningful performance gains, especially in CPU-limited workloads. I still will want to maximize the amount of RAM I have on my personal systems but will also start to tune RAM settings on my client's systems because RAM is starting to become unaffordable for many.

---

### AMD’s 3D V-Cache Is Still the Best Gaming Upgrade
**URL:** https://www.xda-developers.com/amds-3d-v-cache-is-still-the-best-gaming-upgrade-money-can-buy/

AMD’s stacked-cache CPUs continue to dominate gaming benchmarks.

**Commentary:**  
Large on-chip cache reduces memory latency — a critical factor in game engines. It’s a rare case where a single architectural feature consistently delivers real-world benefits. AMD is really giving Intel a run for its money right now.

## Networking & Connectivity

### Five Signs Your Wi-Fi Router Is Too Old (XDA)
**URL:** https://www.xda-developers.com/signs-your-wifi-router-is-too-old/

Common warning signs include missing modern standards, overheating, lack of WPA3, and inability to handle current ISP speeds.

**Commentary:**  
Routers often become silent bottlenecks. Upgrading networking gear frequently delivers a bigger real-world improvement than upgrading endpoint devices.  While the majority of light Internet users will not benefit from the upgrade to a newer router, the security improves make it something that needs to be done. ## Aviation & Geopolitics

### Aeroméxico Proposes Terminal 3 at Mexico City Airport
**URL:** https://mexico-now.com/aeromexico-proposes-terminal-3/

A proposal to expand capacity at Mexico City’s primary airport.

**Commentary:**  
I’ve flown through Terminals 1 and 2 multiple times in recent months and was shocked by the sheer scale of construction underway at both. Large portions of the airport are being renovated simultaneously — from waiting areas and access roads to infrastructure systems.

For years, funding and political attention shifted toward the newer Felipe Ángeles International Airport (AIFA), while the older Mexico City International Airport (AICM) operated with constrained maintenance budgets. In some years, AICM’s funding was cut dramatically even as resources flowed to the newer facility, leading to mounting infrastructure issues. :contentReference[oaicite:0]{index=0}  

More recently, substantial investment has been redirected back into AICM, including large-scale modernization and repair projects ahead of the 2026 World Cup, with billions of pesos allocated for upgrades across both terminals. :contentReference[oaicite:1]{index=1}  

The result is what travelers are seeing now: a massive catch-up effort after years of deferred maintenance. It’s disruptive in the short term but likely necessary to keep the airport operational as demand continues to exceed capacity.

### U.S. Spy Drone Reverse-Engineered by Russia & China
**URL:** https://www.eurasiantimes.com/u-s-spy-drone-reverse-engineered-by-both-russia-china-the-untold-story-of-lockheed-martins-3-d-21/

A retrospective on the Cold War-era D-21 reconnaissance drone.

**Commentary:**  
The D-21 represents a class of experimental systems built when extreme performance was prioritized over practicality. Whether foreign powers extracted meaningful technology from recovered wreckage remains debated, but the program illustrates how far aerospace innovation pushed boundaries during that era.

In Ben Rich's Skunkworks, he commented that a Russian military official gave him a panel found in Siberia that they thought was from the F-117.  It was actually part of a D-21 drone that had been lost years earlier.

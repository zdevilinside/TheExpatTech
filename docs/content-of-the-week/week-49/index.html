Week 49 Highlights

This week brought a variety of stories that resonated with me — from tech debates to lifestyle inspiration.

🌍 Budget-Friendly Asian Countries

Investopedia published a list of 10 affordable Asian destinations where retirees and nomads can stretch their dollars.

  • India and Nepal: ~$270/month living costs.
  • Thailand: ~$600/month with strong healthcare.
  • Vietnam: ~$350/month with UNESCO heritage sites.
    These countries combine affordability with culture and visa options, making them attractive for long-term stays.

💻 USB Port Colors Explained

ZDNet broke down the color coding of USB ports:

  • Black = USB 2.0 (480 Mbps)
  • Blue = USB 3.0 (5 Gbps)
  • Teal = USB 3.1 (10 Gbps)
  • Red = USB 3.2 (20 Gbps, always-on)
    It’s a reminder that not all ports are equal — even identical-looking USB-C ports can differ in speed.

🚗 Tourist Tax in Baja California

FOX LA confirmed that Baja California Sur will continue its mandatory “Embrace It” tourist tax in 2026.

  • Applies to all international travelers over 12.
  • Funds go to environmental protection and tourism infrastructure.
    Over 3.8M foreign tourists visited in 2025, showing strong demand despite the fee.

🎥 Mexican Minimum Wage Increase

Reuters reported Mexico’s 13% minimum wage hike for 2025, aimed at improving worker livelihoods.
This reflects broader Latin American trends of governments pushing wage growth to counter inflation.

🏡 North Macedonia Tourism

CNN Travel highlighted Skopje and North Macedonia as emerging travel destinations, blending Balkan culture, Ottoman history, and affordability.

🤖 Micron & Crucial Price Gouging

The Verge covered Micron/Crucial’s latest RAM/SSD launches.
Here’s the outrage: Micron has taken billions in taxpayer subsidies to build fabs in the U.S. — supposedly to secure supply chains and lower costs. Instead, they’re jacking up consumer prices, hiding behind “AI demand” as an excuse.
We paid for their factories, yet they’re squeezing us at the checkout. This is corporate greed at its worst — subsidized by public money, rewarded with higher costs for everyday consumers.

🌐 GPU Scandal

VideoCardz reported a bizarre case: a Redditor ordered a $1,200 RTX 5080 GPU from Best Buy but received rocks in the box. Even worse, Best Buy’s investigation claimed “no issue.” A surreal reminder of how broken retail logistics can be.

🖥️ Starlink vs. Viasat

BGR compared satellite internet providers:

  • Starlink: faster speeds (up to 270 Mbps) but poor BBB rating (F).
  • Viasat: cheaper residential plans ($69.99–$99.99) but slower speeds (150 Mbps).
    Starlink wins on performance, Viasat on affordability.

📡 Wi-Fi Dead Zone Fix

BGR reviewed the TP-Link RE315 Wi-Fi extender (~$19).

  • Dual-band speeds up to 1,200 Mbps.
  • Supports 30 devices.
  • Easy setup via TP-Link app.
    A cheap fix for spotty home networks.

🛂 Trade Tensions

Reuters reported USTR’s warning that Canada and Mexico shouldn’t become export hubs for China, reflecting ongoing geopolitical trade battles.

🏈 Denver Broncos Nail-Biter Record

Denver Broncos fans almost flat-lined last Sunday night before Nik Bonitto swatted away the 2-point conversion pass in overtime.
The game was back and forth but, like many earlier games this season, the Broncos won by just one score.

They have now set the NFL record with 8 one-score wins in a single season, showing an uncanny ability to survive close contests.

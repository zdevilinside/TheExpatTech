🌍 Budget-Friendly Asian Countries

Investopedia published a list of 10 affordable Asian destinations where retirees and nomads can stretch their dollars.

  • India and Nepal: ~$270/month living costs.
  • Thailand: ~$600/month with strong healthcare.
  • Vietnam: ~$350/month with UNESCO heritage sites.

These countries are ones that Digital Nomads are visiting and loving due to the low cost and the natural beauty. A lot of people are looking to retire here for the same reasons.

💻 USB Port Colors Explained

ZDNet broke down the color coding of USB ports:

  • Black = USB 2.0 (480 Mbps)
  • Blue = USB 3.0 (5 Gbps)
  • Teal = USB 3.1 (10 Gbps)
  • Red = USB 3.2 (20 Gbps, always-on)

I remember when USB 1.0 came out and when USB 2.0 was released. I never paid attention to the colors of the port and always wondered what they meant. Now I do!

🚗 Tourist Tax in Baja California

FOX LA confirmed that Baja California Sur will continue its mandatory “Embrace It” tourist tax in 2026.

  • Applies to all international travelers over 12.
  • Must be paid online before or upon arrival.
  • Cost: 470 pesos per person (~$25–27 USD).
  • This tax is in addition to the normal FMM (Forma Migratoria Múltiple) charge, which costs 861 pesos (~$45 USD) as of 2025.

That means a visitor is paying 1,331 pesos total (~$70 USD) in entry-related fees. It’s not pocket change, especially if you’re traveling as a family. Mexico is smart to invest these funds back into its environment and tourism, but travelers should budget for both fees when planning their trip.

Over 3.8M foreign tourists visited in 2025, showing strong demand despite the fee.

🎥 Mexican Minimum Wage Increase

Reuters reported Mexico’s 13% minimum wage hike for 2025.

Mexico has increased the minimum wage from 70.1 Pesos a day back in 2015 to $315.04 a day. That might sound like a lot but when you factor in the exchange rate, it is $17.33 A DAY. Now you know why so many head north of the border to work.

🏡 North Macedonia - A Tourist’s Delight

CNN Travel highlighted Skopje and North Macedonia as emerging travel destinations, blending Balkan culture, Ottoman history, and affordability. Part of Alexander The Great’s Empire, it became a separate country when Yugoslavia broke up. It is overlooked as a destination for many but that is slowly changing. Go to this golden nugget before it is found by the masses.

🎶 Music Week – Earworms & Soundtracks

It was a music week for me. Between movies, nostalgia, and random earworms, these tracks kept looping in my head and reminded me how much music shapes the mood of everything around us.

  • Greg Kihn Band – Breakup Song (They Don’t Write ’Em)
    I didn’t realize until now that Greg Kihn passed away in August 2024 at the age of 75, after a battle with Alzheimer’s. His music was part of the soundtrack of the early MTV era, and this song in particular is one of those timeless earworms that sticks with you. Proof that some songs really don’t fade, even if “they don’t write ’em like that anymore.”

  • INXS – To Look At You
    I became amazed by the power of this song after watching the INXS documentary Never Tear Us Apart. Michael Hutchence and the band were doing okay until one night they were playing in a bar. Michael looked out at the audience and found Michelle. He started to “peacock” to the song — strutting, commanding the stage — and the crowd went wild. That moment showed how a single performance can transform a song into something unforgettable.

  • Frida – I Know There’s Something Going On
    I’ve loved this song ever since I first heard it in the movie Just The Way You Are. What really struck me was recognizing Phil Collins’ backing vocals and his unmistakable drumming — it gives the track that haunting, driving pulse that makes it unforgettable.

  • Peter Schilling – Major Tom (Coming Home)

  • New Order – Blue Monday

  • Re-Flex – The Politics of Dancing

  • The Clash – London Calling

  • A Flock of Seagulls – I Ran
    (All featured in Atomic Blonde — a movie where the soundtrack drives the action as much as the visuals.)

  • INXS – The One Thing
    Another INXS track that’s been stuck in my head. It’s got that raw energy that made them stand out in the early 80s, and it still feels fresh today.

  • Chris Daughtry & Lzzy Hale – Separate Ways (Worlds Apart)
    I grew up with Journey’s version, and Lzzy Hale’s powerhouse vocals made me fall in love with it all over again. Daughtry takes it further — this cover is an out‑of‑the‑park smash that proves some songs can be reborn with even more fire.

  • Kate Bush & David Gilmour – Running Up That Hill (Live)
    I love the heavier sound of this version, with three greats on stage: Kate Bush, David Gilmour, and — let’s not forget — that other guy’s hairdresser. ;) :P It’s a performance that blends artistry, grit, and a touch of humor, and it hits harder than the studio cut.

  • Lasgo – Surrender
    This one takes me straight back to the early 2000s trance/eurodance era. Surrender has that soaring vocal and pulsing beat that makes it impossible not to get swept up in it. It’s pure energy, and it still feels fresh today.

  • Loverboy – Turn Me Loose

  • Krokus – Screaming in the Night (Acoustic)
    These two songs carry a very personal weight for me. Years ago, I lost my girlfriend in a car accident while we were on the phone. She was using a hands‑free device when it happened, and I didn’t find out until the next day. I was barely functioning, but these tracks — Loverboy’s raw defiance in Turn Me Loose and Krokus’ haunting acoustic version of Screaming in the Night — helped me claw my way back. Music can’t erase pain, but sometimes it gives you just enough strength to keep moving.

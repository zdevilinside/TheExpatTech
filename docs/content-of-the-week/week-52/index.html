Week 52 - These are the things I found interesting

🏠 Personal Update

This is my last week away from home for a while. I’m heading back on Friday and will finally be back to normal. While I love traveling to spend time with my daughter, I still prefer being home. I miss my fur balls, the familiar weather, and the comfort of my own space — it gets quite cold here!

💻 Alienware Adventures (Again…)

I’ve also had an “interesting” week with my Alienware laptop. It stopped POSTing and needed a motherboard replacement. After the new motherboard was installed, one of the memory modules failed. While waiting for the replacement module, I discovered that the new motherboard’s NIC didn’t recognize any Ethernet cables.

When I contacted Dell support, they insisted the tech replacing the memory should also verify the NIC failure — the classic “replace a tire and pray it fixes the transmission” routine. Unfortunately, this is nothing new.

Over the years, I’ve had a revolving door of Dell and Alienware systems, all of them top‑tier models, and all of them failing in one way or another:

  • Discrete GPUs that HWInfo32 reported as “unknown”, including the GPU’s onboard RAM
  • Multiple failed motherboards
  • Hard drives dying prematurely
  • A Wi‑Fi card that suddenly stopped being recognized
  • Heat issues that likely contributed to most of these failures

I earned my first Dell certification back in 1999 and have been in the industry ever since. It’s pretty sad that they still won’t listen to me and simply send a replacement motherboard when the issue is obvious.

🖥️ IT World Stories Worth Sharing

🧯 Microsoft’s December Patch Wave — A Rough One

This month’s patch cycle hit harder than usual. Several enterprises reported:

  • Broken VPN clients
  • Failed RDP sessions
  • Authentication loops with Azure AD
  • A nasty bug that caused Windows Server 2022 to reboot under heavy load

It’s a reminder that patching is both essential and dangerous — a tightrope walk every sysadmin knows too well.

🧠 AI Model Supply Chain Issues

AI hardware shortages continued this week, with multiple cloud providers quietly admitting they’re rationing GPU time.

This is the new oil crisis — except instead of gas lines, we get:

  • Delayed training jobs
  • Higher inference costs
  • “GPU hoarding” by big players

It’s wild to see how fast the world shifted from “AI is neat” to “AI is infrastructure.”

🛡️ Major Password Manager Breach Fallout

The long tail of the password manager breach from earlier this year is still hitting users. More reports surfaced of:

  • Crypto wallet drains
  • Account takeovers
  • SIM swap attempts

It’s a brutal reminder:
If your vault was compromised, rotate everything.
Painful, but necessary.

🔧 Final Thoughts — And One Last Word About Dell

Before wrapping up this week’s digest, I have to circle back to Dell’s support logic — or lack thereof.

It still amazes me that after decades in the industry, multiple certifications, and a long history of hardware failures across top‑tier Dell and Alienware systems, their support process continues to rely on the “replace a tire and hope it fixes the transmission” philosophy.

It’s exhausting.
It’s wasteful.
And it’s exactly why so many people lose faith in big‑brand support.

Here’s hoping next week brings fewer motherboard swaps and more common sense.
